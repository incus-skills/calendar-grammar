If you **perturb** a **driver** in **Domain A** (e.g., remove the temperature gradient in the distillation column) and the **oscillation** **vanishes**, but the **same** perturbation in **Domain B** (e.g., remove the debt cycle in the financial market) **does not** **vanish** the **oscillation** (because a **different** driver is sustaining it), then the **behaviors are NOT homologous** — they are **convergent**.

**Convergent behaviors** are **fragile**: they **persist** only as long as the **drivers** persist. If the **drivers change**, the **behavior vanishes**.

**Homologous behaviors** are **robust**: they **persist** across **variations** in the **drivers** because the **mechanism** is **invariant**.

## Implication for the Affinity Attractor Framework

The **Affinity Attractor** must be **parameterized** not just by the **structural features** (orthogonal coupling, graded boundaries, hysteresis) but by the **functional drivers**:

| Domain                  | Structural Feature          | Functional Driver                                                   | Homology Test                                                            |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Distillation Column** | Stratification, oscillation | Temperature gradient, gravity, composition gradient                 | **Perturb** the gradient → **oscillation vanishes**                      |
| **Financial Market**    | Debt cycle oscillation      | Debt-to-GDP ratio, sentiment feedback, liquidity                    | **Perturb** the debt cycle → **oscillation persists** (different driver) |
| **Social System**       | Polarization oscillation    | Algorithmic amplification, identity polarization, resource scarcity | **Perturb** the algorithm → **oscillation vanishes**                     |

The **homology** is **confirmed** only if the **same** **perturbation** (e.g., **removing the throughput**) **vanishes** the **behavior** in **both** domains. If the **perturbation** **vanishes** the **behavior** in **one** domain but **not** the other, the **behaviors are convergent**, not **homologous**.

## The Engineering Discipline: Refined Axiom

**Axiom 6: Functional Homology**

> A cross-domain analogy is **valid** only if the **structural features** (oscillation, hysteresis, stratification) are **sustained** by **functionally homologous drivers** (throughput, restoring forces, coupling functions). **Convergent behaviors** (same form, different drivers) are **fragile** and **vanish** when the **drivers change**. **Homologous behaviors** (same form, same drivers) are **robust** and **persist** across **variations** in the **drivers**.

The **testable method** is **driver perturbation**: **remove** or **modify** a **driver** in **one** domain and **observe** if the **behavior** **persists** in **both** domains. If it **persists** in **both**, the **drivers are homologous**. If it **vanishes** in **one** but **not** the other, the **drivers are different**, and the **analogy is convergent**, not **homologous**.
