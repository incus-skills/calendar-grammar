# Childrens Tales

I have no idea if these are true preservation artifacts but would like to use them as vehicles for the application of generative grammars to reframe them as preservation artifacts.

They are childrens tales so should translate into a number of substraits.

## Y Mabinogi and other Welsh tales

The Mabinogion is a collection of 11 medieval Welsh prose stories compiled in the 12th–13th centuries from earlier oral traditions. ￼ These tales, preserved primarily in the White Book of Rhydderch (c. ￼ 1350) and the Red Book of Hergest (c. ￼ 1382–1410), blend Celtic mythology, folklore, and Arthurian legend.

You should decide if you want:

a.) a literal translation: in that case, go with the Jones and Jones translation of the 1950s (IIRC), offered by Everyman
<https://www.amazon.co.uk/Mabinogion-Everymans-Library-Classics/dp/1857151682>

b.) a readable translation that also tries to give the flavor of the medieval original: in that case, go with Sioned Davies' translation from 2006.
<https://www.amazon.co.uk/Mabinogion-Oxford-Worlds-Classics/dp/0199218781>
We can combine the notes and the prose for the semantic compression de-layering.

c.) a translation that focuses on the pre-Christian mythology of the non-Romance tales: in that case, go with the Patrick Ford translation from the 1970s. The advantage of Ford's translation is its inclusion of the earliest version of "The Story of Taliesin"; the disadvantage is it doesn't include the Three Romances ("Peredur", "Owain", and "Gereint").
<https://www.amazon.co.uk/Mabinogi-Other-Medieval-Welsh-Tales/dp/0520309588>

d.) a translation that focuses on the environment of Wales: the Bollard translation is great for this.
<https://www.amazon.co.uk/Mabinogi-John-Bollard/dp/1843233487>
