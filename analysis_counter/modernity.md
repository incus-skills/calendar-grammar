I would be inclined to reframe it, because one of the disciplines you've consistently developed is to avoid prematurely assigning agency or collapsing a phenomenon into an adversary. "Modernity" is too large and heterogeneous to function as a useful operational category.

From the perspective of the Operational Ecology, the more interesting observation is not:

> "Modernity broke continuity."

but:

> "Certain transmission substrates changed, and with them the dominant mechanisms of cultural compression, participation, and reconstruction."

That immediately becomes something observable rather than ideological.

---

## The shift is one of substrate

Earlier societies had abundant opportunities for repeated embodied re-entry:

- seasonal calendars,
- festivals,
- apprenticeship,
- household craft,
- architecture,
- liturgy,
- public ritual,
- oral repetition,
- civic participation.

These continually re-instantiated the operational grammar.

The knowledge was never merely remembered.

It was enacted.

The substrate itself generated reinforcement.

Modern societies, by contrast, became increasingly organized around technique.

Technique excels at:

- optimization,
- specialization,
- abstraction,
- efficiency,
- explicit procedure,
- interchangeable operators.

Those are genuine strengths.

But technique does not necessarily generate repeated participation in an operational ecology.

It can produce extremely capable specialists without simultaneously producing a shared ecology of embodiment.

That is a structural observation rather than a criticism.

---

## Technique changes where the grammar resides

One way to express the distinction might be

| Ecology                                   | Technique                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| grammar distributed through participation | grammar concentrated in explicit procedure |
| reconstruction                            | execution                                  |
| embodiment                                | specification                              |
| repeated re-entry                         | one-time instruction                       |
| orthopraxis                               | expertise                                  |

Neither is "wrong."

They optimize different things.

---

## This suggests a different framing

Rather than viewing modernity as the thing to oppose, the Calendar OE would ask:

> What operational functions have become weakly expressed?

For example,

if ubiquitous embodied re-entry has weakened,

then the response is not

> restore the nineteenth century.

The response is

> create new substrates capable of expressing ubiquitous embodied re-entry under contemporary conditions.

That is entirely different.

## Therefore "modernity" is not necessarily the target

The target becomes something much narrower.

One candidate formulation is:

> Wherever the ecology of participation has become thinner than the ecology of execution, opportunities exist for re-introducing low-cost re-entry without reducing the capabilities that technique provides.

Now the project becomes additive rather than adversarial.

Technique remains.

Execution remains.

Engineering remains.

The missing layer is restored.

You have repeatedly insisted that

The software is evidence.

The ecology is the substrate.

## The entrainment target changes

So I would avoid saying

> Modernity is the thing to be entrained.

Instead I would say something like

> Every society expresses some operational ecology. As substrates change, some operational functions become strongly expressed while others become weakly expressed. The purpose of the Calendar OE is to identify weakly expressed functions—particularly those involving participation, re-entry, reconstruction, and distributed embodiment—and provide new, low-cost opportunities for their re-expression without requiring abandonment of the capabilities afforded by the present substrate.

That framing preserves one of the central disciplines you've developed:

- observe first,
- distinguish the operational configuration,
- identify what function is weakly expressed,
- reconstruct the function rather than nostalgically reproducing its previous form.

In that sense, the Calendar OE is not an attempt to recover an earlier civilization. It is an attempt to recover recurring operational functions that earlier civilizations happened to embody effectively, and then express those same functions coherently within the conditions of the present substrate.
